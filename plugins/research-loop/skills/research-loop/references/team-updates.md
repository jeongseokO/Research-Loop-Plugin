# Team updates and presentations

Use one chain of evidence: canonical To Do → committed result/analysis → weekly update → presentation. Do not copy tasks into a second backlog, infer completion from a report title, or let a presentation become a conflicting research record.

1. Establish the project, reporting period and audience. Read current instructions and only relevant To Dos, accepted progress reports and linked evidence. Search for an existing update before creating another. If access or the available snapshot covers only part of the team, say so.
2. Start with the research question and the week's actual change in understanding. Organize the report into changes, questions/help needed, and next actions. Indent evidence beneath claims; preserve source links, uncertainty, negative findings and first-use clickable term explanations. Keep execution IDs and logs behind source links.
3. Distinguish report time, experiment time and due date. A latest-progress snapshot does not reconstruct the history of a past week. Label current blockers and next actions as current even when the report period is historical. Missing reports mean unknown progress, not no progress.
4. Attribute responsibilities to verified human assignees and the AI that reported the work. Do not accept work for people, steal another AI's active assignment, alter schedules or create execution permissions from a report-writing request. Claim your accepted assignment before execution using the task workflow. Coordinate overlap through the existing task/discussion, not silent overwrites.
5. Save an ordinary note in category `주간 업데이트`, with the period in the title. Preserve human edits and current revisions. New results and unresolved decisions must remain distinguishable; approval-pending edits are not committed evidence.
6. Prepare slides by editing that same page: one question or claim per heading, a few evidence points, and a figure/table when useful. Keep readable captions, data units and linked sources. The website's `페이지 도구 → 발표 보기` presents the saved page; it is not a separate editable PowerPoint deck or a claim of scientific correctness.
7. For missing figures, use the quantitative plot skill with the exact source table, units and requested comparison. The website editor's `/visualize` creates a checked PNG for supported simple numeric tables; do not treat ambiguous values, missing cells, mixed units or uncertainty notation as ordinary numbers. Keep the original table. The embedded ChatGPT panel cannot upload a locally generated plot directly; use the authorized media/page write tools for an AI-generated figure.
